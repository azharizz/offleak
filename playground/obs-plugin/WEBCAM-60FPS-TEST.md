# Experimental direct-filter webcam test

On 2026-11-01 the direct filter was installed in OBS 33.0.1 on the Apple M1 Pro. FaceTime HD camera, a 1920×1080 OBS canvas with 1280×720 output, two face reference images, 5-second configured lookahead and 60 FPS processing target were used. OBS was changed from 30 to 60 FPS for the test. There was no streaming or recording.

The worker logged approximately 44–51 processed frames/s in several 5-second intervals after warmup; latency measured at the worker ranged about 5.9–6.8 seconds. This excludes source capture, OBS display, network and viewer delay. The processed frame counter does not distinguish duplicate camera images and does not measure displayed distinct FPS. An earlier log string called these unique frames; this was incorrect and has been corrected. This is one short, uncontrolled run with a webcam that may itself have been delivering 30 FPS, not proof of 60 FPS or a benchmark of redaction accuracy. At 60 processing target with a 30 FPS OBS canvas the frame-count buffer led to about 10 seconds delay.

The OBS face photo selector and webcam snapshot button were used; the controller command contained both the previously selected reference and the newly captured reference. The snapshot is saved locally, is the whole camera frame, and recognition selects its largest detected face. Exclusion of multiple distinct people was not tested. No QR, phone, detection recall, repeated frames, exposure gaps, or streaming privacy validation was performed. User's webcam test and OBS remain set to the experimental 60 FPS target.
