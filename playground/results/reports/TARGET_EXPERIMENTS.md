# Privacy-priority experiments

These experiments target fewer selected-person false blurs and fewer QR/phone misses. They are offline, experimental, and not production safe. They do not demonstrate 70% person accuracy or 99% QR/phone recall on real IRL footage. No audio is preserved.

## Face: guarded exclusion

`guarded_face.py` uses the existing first-frame selection, automatic person tracking/reacquisition, and cached Vision+YuNet face boxes. It extends other-face masks for 0.5s before/after each frame. To reduce spill onto the selected-person proxy, it clears a region around that proxy only if the entire proposed clear area does not intersect any *currently detected non-selected* face box. No manually annotated positions are used after the first selection.

On this clip, for the heuristic-selected proxy in 575 of 620 frames, more than 10% of the proxy area was masked in 296 frames before the guard and 101 after it. This proxy was generated by the same dominant-face heuristic being tested, may represent the wrong person, excludes 45 frames with no proxy, and is not a labeled 82% person accuracy result. All 2,474 detected boxes classified as other faces had at least 90% of their box area covered by the generated mask; this is a box-geometry self-check, not proof that no bystanders are visible. Selected-person mix-ups, detected faces classified as selected, undetected faces, incomplete boxes, and recoverable blur remain possible. Spot checks still show blur on the selected person and visible bystanders. Clearing pixels can expose an undetected face. Never use this as a live privacy filter. Preview: `playground/results/playable/face-guarded.mp4`.

## QR

`qr_groundtruth_test.py` evaluated all 90 frames of the simple 640x360 synthetic moving QR test with known 200x200 QR rectangles. QRDet and Apple Vision, individually and together, masked at least 99% of the known rectangle in 90/90 frames. An OpenCV reader decoded 90/90 source frames and 0/90 blurred frames in that easy test. This is one synthetic code, not a 99% real-world result.

`qr_stress_test.py` overlaid a moving known QR on real video at 24, 40, 80 and 140 pixels, 30 frames each, with synthetic blur on every third frame. Without hold, the QRDet+Vision union covered >=99% of the known rectangle in 20/30 frames at 24px and 30/30 at each larger size. The OpenCV reader decoded none of the original 24px or 40px images, so blur resistance was not testable at those sizes. At 80px and 140px it decoded the original on 19 and 29 frames respectively and decoded none of the corresponding blurred outputs. These are small synthetic samples with fixed format; rectangle mask coverage is not true scannability guarantee.

`qr_small_scale_test.py` reran the 30 24px examples at 1280 and 1920 model input size: 20/30 versus 23/30 had >=99% known rectangle coverage; median model times were 228.64ms and 374.24ms respectively, with other tasks competing for CPU. This did not reach 99%; larger inference costs more time. On the real 20–23s excerpt, adding 1920 inference to the cached QRDet+WeChat union increased frames with at least one detection from 67 to 68 of 75. The number of candidates is not recall and does not say whether both codes were covered. `qr_upscaled.py` renders `playground/results/playable/qr-upscaled.mp4` using all candidates and 0.5s look-ahead. Its new 1920 pass had a 384.21ms median in a concurrent run, not a live latency benchmark. The original source/blurred sample at 20.5s was visually checked and the QR crop contrast reduced, but no independently readable original example was established in this real excerpt.

## Phone

`phone_stress_test.py` placed known text rectangles of four font scales on a real-video background, 30 frames each, with input blur on a third of frames. At the smallest 0.35 font scale, fast OCR covered >=99% of the rectangle in 0/30, accurate OCR in 18/30, upscaled 2x accurate OCR in 14/30, and their union in 21/30. The union covered 30/30 for each of the three larger scales. This small synthetic set clearly does not demonstrate 99% phone recall and does not prove blurred digits cannot be recovered.

`independent_phone_audit.py` used RapidOCR (different from Apple Vision used for masking) on all 360 frames of the real 73–85s phone excerpt: it found a phone-like candidate in 302 original frames and 0 blurred frames. It checks one screen crop and one parser, not ground truth, other formats, or all possible OCR/readers. OCR values are not saved. `phone_upscaled.py` adds a 2x accurate-OCR pass to the cached fast+accurate detections and renders `playground/results/playable/phone-upscaled.mp4` with 0.5s look-ahead. On this excerpt the added pass increased frames with any candidate from 290 to 291 of 360; this is not recall. An independent OCR recheck was done on the preceding union preview, not this new version; source and output sample at 80s were visually checked and the number crop contrast reduced.

## Latency and reproducibility

A 0.5s look-ahead requires at least 0.5 seconds before any model or encoding/network time. The new 1920 pass alone was much slower than a 25fps frame budget in this experiment; adding detectors is not proof of a lightweight system. No camera-to-output or phone-device measurements were made. The QRDet/Ultralytics AGPL licensing issue remains; see COMPARISON.md.

Existing venv requires PyObjC, OpenCV contrib, NumPy, QRDet/Ultralytics, and RapidOCR. Inputs and cached detection files are under `playground`.

```sh
playground/.venv/bin/python playground/scripts/guarded_face.py
playground/.venv/bin/python playground/scripts/qr_groundtruth_test.py
playground/.venv/bin/python playground/scripts/qr_stress_test.py
playground/.venv/bin/python playground/scripts/qr_small_scale_test.py
playground/.venv/bin/python playground/scripts/phone_stress_test.py
playground/.venv/bin/python playground/scripts/independent_phone_audit.py
playground/.venv/bin/python playground/scripts/qr_upscaled.py
playground/.venv/bin/python playground/scripts/phone_upscaled.py
```

A production claim needs independently annotated diverse real video, per-frame and per-appearance recall, wrong-person exemption tests, scanner/OCR attack tests, identity-switch analysis, synchronized handling when detectors lag or fail, and live target-device latency/resource tests. Since any exposure may matter, fail closed rather than extrapolating a percentage from these examples.
